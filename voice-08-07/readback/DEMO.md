# The 60-second demo

Setup (before the clock): `go run .`, open http://localhost:8014, sound on.

**0:00 — one sentence.** "Voice ops isn't unsafe because recognition is
imperfect; it's unsafe without readback. Aviation solved this decades ago.
Watch."

**0:05 — click ▶ canned demo (no mic).** Narrate over it:

**Scene 1 (clean order).** "I say *deploy checkout to production*. It reads
back the exact parsed action — version spelled digit by digit, 'to
PRODUCTION' stressed — and hands me a confirm token. I echo the token, and
only then does production change. Left panel: prod just went v13."

**0:25 — Scene 2 (the mishear — the whole product).** "Now I say *merge
roxiq PR fourteen* — and the mic hears **forty**. Watch the readback: it
says 'P R **four zero**'. That's not what I said, so I say **negative**.
Nothing executed. The mishear cost nothing, and the audit trail shows it:
armed, cancelled, zero effect."

**0:40 — Scene 3 (ambiguity).** "I say just *merge roxiq* while two PRs are
open. It won't guess — it asks, out loud: 'one four and four zero, say the
number.' I say it fully, confirm one four, merged."

**0:50 — point at the audit trail.** "Every exchange: heard text → parsed
action → readback → confirm → effect, timestamped. This is the change-
management evidence an auditor asks for, generated by the safety loop
itself."

**0:58 — close.** "I just told it to merge PR fourteen; it heard forty; it
cost nothing. This is the readback."

Live-mic bonus (if there's time): hit 🎤 and speak `flag dark mode on`, then
try `confirm` with the *wrong* number first — it refuses — then the right
one. Stale-confirm party trick: say `confirm one four` with nothing armed.

## Would someone pay

**Buyer: the platform/SRE team that already runs ChatOps deploys and pays
for incident tooling** (PagerDuty, incident.io, FireHydrant — real budgets,
renewed annually). They already gate irreversible actions behind typed
confirmations in Slack; the moment those workflows go voice — on-call in a
car, hands-full incident response, ambient agents — they need exactly this
layer, because a voice interface without readback is a production incident
with a latency of one mishear. Second buyer: compliance. The audit trail
(heard → readback → confirm → effect) is SOC 2 change-management evidence
produced as a side effect of the safety mechanism.

The push-back I expect: "nobody drives deploys by voice today." Right — and
nobody merged from Slack until ChatOps made it safe enough to be normal.
The readback loop is the thing that makes voice ops *allowed to exist*.
Voice-necessity: this entry isn't a voice skin on a text app — the entire
mechanism (spelled digits, spoken confirm token, hearback) only means
anything on an audio channel.

## What's deliberately not here

No LLM anywhere in the command path (there is no model at all — the grammar
is a closed set). No real git/gh/deploy integration. No accounts, no RBAC,
no config, no framework, no JS build, no dependencies. One Go binary, one
embedded HTML file, ~1,800 lines total including all tests and the UI.
